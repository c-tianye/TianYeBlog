## Overview

This issue highlights recent preprints from arXiv, exploring multi-modal signal understanding, consolidating agent experiences into model weights, reverse-engineering parameters into text, and memory-efficient LLM inference.

## Papers

### [LEMMO: A Large Electromagnetic Model leveraging Pretrained LLMs for Million-Sample Signal Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09476v1)

A new preprint paper introduces LEMMO to tackle raw electromagnetic (EM) signal understanding across long recordings with millions of samples. By extending pretrained LLMs into the EM modality, it addresses the limitations of traditional segment-based waveform models.

### [ENGRAM: From Agent Experience to Weights through Verified Consolidation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09472v1)

A new paper explores how language-model agents can consolidate external retrieval experiences into model weights. The authors introduce a verified consolidation framework to resolve when experience should be trained on and safely removed from external memory.

### [From LoRA to Language: Recovering Skill Documents from Adapters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09454v1)

Addressing the unreadability and edit difficulty of LoRA adapters, a new paper investigates recovering human-readable skill documents directly from low-rank adapters and their frozen base models.

### [MINE: Entropy-coded Memory-efficient Inference for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09424v1)

To overcome the memory-bound constraints of single-stream LLM decoding, a new paper proposes MINE, a lossless entropy coding inference engine. It accelerates single-stream decoding across general data types without needing restrictive assumptions.
