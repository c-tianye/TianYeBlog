## Overview

This edition reviews the latest research preprints from arXiv, spotlighting advances in embodied AI, neural speech decoding, mechanistic interpretability, and wafer-scale hardware acceleration. Key highlights include training open-weight reasoning models natively for robotics without real-world demonstrations, alongside novel benchmarks for direct weight-space manipulation.

## Papers

### [Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562v1)

This new preprint demonstrates that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained via pure simulation reinforcement learning to operate physical robots natively, narrowing the performance gap with proprietary frontier models.

### [FETTUCCINE: Fast and efficient brain-to-text decoding on mobile devices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18561v1)

Addressing the prohibitive computational overhead of speech neuroprostheses, this preprint proposes an efficient neural decoding framework tailored for mobile hardware to deliver real-time communication.

### [WeightBench: Evaluating Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18553v1)

This paper formalizes the evaluation of large language models acting as weight-space programmers capable of inspecting, fixing, and synthesizing other neural network parameters without gradient descent.

### [ModHarness-RL: Recasting Harnesses as Learnable and Evolving Programs for Agentic RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18533v1)

Challenging static scaffolding in agentic reinforcement learning, this preprint recasts agent interaction harnesses as learnable, evolving programs that dynamically adjust during model training.

### [WaferTetris: Efficient Freeform Mapping on Wafer-Scale Accelerators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18527v1)

This preprint introduces a freeform mapping technique designed to unlock massive compute utilization across hundreds of thousands of processing elements on wafer-scale architectures beyond rigid template constraints.
