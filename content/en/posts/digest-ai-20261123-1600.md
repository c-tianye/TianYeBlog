## Overview

This issue covers recent preprint papers featuring breakthroughs in LLM architecture optimization, multimodal physical reasoning, and AI for science. Key updates explore scalable long-horizon memory, self-optimizing network topologies during Transformer training, and binocular 3D spatial reasoning for vision-language models.

## Papers

### [MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27973v1)

Standard Transformers suffer from linear KV cache growth during long-horizon interactions; a new preprint paper introduces MemOnce to compress dialogue history into reusable memory states for efficient inference.

### [Model Carving: Training Transformers That Optimize Their Own Topology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27962v1)

Classical Transformers rely on fixed ordering of attention and MLP sublayers, which may be suboptimal; a recent preprint proposes Model Carving, allowing networks to dynamically optimize their own topology during training.

### [BioEmu2: Scalable Emulation of Biomolecular Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27924v1)

Modeling biomolecular function remains computationally expensive; a new paper introduces BioEmu2, a generative model that combines static structure prediction and equilibrium sampling to simulate dynamics orders of magnitude faster than conventional molecular dynamics.

### [StereoVLM: Binocular Vision-Language Models for Metric 3D Spatial Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27915v1)

Existing VLMs struggle with metric 3D physical reasoning; researchers present a preprint introducing binocular vision and geometric constraints to ground multimodal models in metric 3D space.

### [Non-penetrating World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27963v1)

To address physical anomalies where objects penetrate each other in video world models, a new preprint introduces the NoPeBench benchmark to evaluate and mitigate object penetration failures.
