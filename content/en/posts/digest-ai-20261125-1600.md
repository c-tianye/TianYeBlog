## Overview

This edition of AI Digest highlights recent research breakthroughs across embodied AI, audiovisual multimodal models, long-horizon policy distillation, and LLM inference optimization. Newly published preprints propose novel methods for cross-embodiment control, structural supervision from scientific literature, and geometrically constrained autoregressive decoding.

## Papers

### [OmniAct: One Policy, One Million Embodiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33158v1)

A new preprint introduces OmniAct, a cross-embodiment manipulation policy designed to control diverse robots by explicitly accounting for how morphological differences shape manipulation strategies.

### [DiaCap: Jointly Learning Who Speaks When for Audiovisual Dialogue Captioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33153v1)

A new paper presents DiaCap, an audiovisual LLM that unifies utterance timing, speaker diarization, visual descriptions, and text transcription into a single model trained via a two-stage approach.

### [BioLoom: Scaling Biomedical Vision-Language Models by Learning from the Structure of Scientific Articles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33151v1)

Researchers propose BioLoom in a recent preprint, demonstrating that leveraging the intrinsic structure of scientific articles—such as figure-panel-caption layouts—provides strong self-supervision to scale biomedical vision-language representations.

### [CoT-Proxy: Cross-Format Transfer for Distilling Long-Horizon Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33148v1)

A new paper addresses policy distillation from closed teacher models whose chain-of-thought is hidden behind APIs, proposing CoT-Proxy for cross-format transfer without requiring explicit teacher thinking tokens.

### [BriConstrain: Geometrically Constrained Decoding for Autoregressive Brick Assembly Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33135v1)

To prevent LLMs from generating colliding or floating 3D brick assemblies, a new preprint introduces BriConstrain, a constrained decoding technique that restricts next-token probabilities to ensure geometrically valid structures.

### [A Bias in Disguise: Tiny Gradient Edits Stop a Small Language Model's Thinking Loops and Complement a Frontier Model's Weight Edit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33133v1)

A new paper investigates thinking loops in small language models, demonstrating that combining tiny gradient edits with weight edits prevents infinite reasoning cycles and boosts accuracy.
