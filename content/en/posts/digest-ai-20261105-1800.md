## Overview

This batch of recent preprints explores the practical deployment of AI agents in scientific research and embodied control. Researchers introduce new evaluation benchmarks for physics and chemistry domains while tackling core efficiency bottlenecks in agent context compaction and inference scaling.

## Papers

### [FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910v1)

A new preprint introduces FrontierPhysics, an end-to-end benchmark spanning 9 domains to evaluate AI agents on full-workflow physics research capabilities and output quality.

### [BEAKER: An Expert-Curated Benchmark for Embodied Brains in Self-Driving Chemical Laboratories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06911v1)

Addressing real-world laboratory automation, a new paper presents BEAKER, an expert-curated benchmark designed to evaluate multimodal LLMs acting as embodied brains under safety and physical constraints.

### [SpecCompact: Accelerating LLM Agent Context Compaction with Slot-Parallel Semantic Speculative Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06876v1)

To address high latency when summarizing long interaction histories in LLM agents, a new paper proposes SpecCompact, leveraging slot-parallel semantic speculative verification for faster context compaction.

### [On Turn-Level Best-of-N Sampling for Scaling LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06868v1)

Targeting complex long-horizon tasks, a new preprint investigates turn-level Best-of-N sampling using a generator-selector framework to scale LLM agents effectively at inference time.

### [Reinforcement Learning Enables Scaling Generalist Robot Policy Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06905v1)

A new paper demonstrates how reinforcement learning enables iterated self-improvement for generalist robot policies, easing the adaptation phase required before deployment.
